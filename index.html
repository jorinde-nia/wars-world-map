About this map

Sources

  • Uppsala Conflict Data Program (UCDP), Uppsala University, Sweden. Georeferenced Event Dataset version 26.1 for 1989 to 2025, and the Candidate Events Dataset up to version 26.0.10 for 2026 (to 5 November). Every event of organised violence with at least one death: state-based conflict, non-state conflict and one-sided violence against civilians. Licence CC BY 4.0.
  • Armed Conflict Location & Event Data (ACLED). Aggregated data by week and province, all regions, 2018 to the week of 7 November 2026. Political violence only: battles, explosions and remote violence, and violence against civilians.
  • GDELT Project. GDELT 1.0 daily event files, coded automatically from news reports worldwide. A sample of two days per month, the 1st and the 15th, from January 2015 to 15 November 2026.
  • Correlates of War Project. Militarized Interstate Disputes version 5.0, 1816 to 2014. Threats, displays and uses of force between states.
  • United Nations Secretary-General. Children and armed conflict, A/80/723–S/2026/357, 16 June 2026. The situations in which the UN monitored grave violations against children in 2025.
  • African Union Peace and Security Council. Provisional programme of work for November 2026.
  • Foreign ministries of Brazil, China, Germany, the Netherlands, Russia and the United States. Travel advice as published on their official websites. Only the two highest levels of each scale are shown.
  • My view. Drawn by hand by Jorinde Hoek from news reports. Approximate.

Method for the fighting regions

  • Each UCDP event is spread over the map as a Gaussian kernel of 60 km, or 110 km when the event is placed only within a province or near a named place.
  • Events placed only within a country, or at sea, are left out.
  • Deaths are UCDP's best estimate, summed per 1,000 km² for each calendar year. Red is 5 deaths or more, light red 0.8 or more. Areas under 3,000 km² are left out. 2026 covers part of the year only.
  • An arrow is drawn when the government of one country is a party to fighting on another country's territory, against that country's government, a group there or civilians, with 25 or more deaths in the year. It runs from the capital to the death-weighted centre of those events. When several governments fight together, each is counted with the full number of deaths.

Method for political violence (ACLED)

  • ACLED's free data gives deaths per province and week, placed at the centre of each province. Each province is spread as a Gaussian kernel of 110 km, with the same thresholds as UCDP: red is 5 deaths or more per 1,000 km², light red 0.8 or more.
  • Protests, riots and strategic developments are left out. There are no arrows, because the aggregated data does not name the sides.

Method for fighting in the news (GDELT)

  • Events with CAMEO root codes 19 (fight) and 20 (mass violence) are kept when they are placed at a town or city and one side is coded as a military, rebel, insurgent, separatist or other armed group.
  • Each place is spread as a Gaussian kernel of 60 km. Density is the share of the year's reports per 1,000 km², so years with more news are comparable with years with less. Red is 2 basis points or more, light red 0.4 or more.
  • An arrow is drawn when a country, its government or its military is reported using force in another country, against that country or an unnamed side, in 30 or more reports and at least 0.2% of all reports of force placed at a town or city that year. Arrows are left out when three-quarters or more of the reports are placed in the target's capital, where news is often filed.

Method for disputes between states (Correlates of War)

  • Countries are coloured by the highest hostility level they reached in any dispute active that year: 5 is war, 4 is use of force. Lower levels (threats and displays of force) are not shown.
  • Arrows run from capital to capital, from the side that took the first militarized action (side A) to the other side, for disputes at level 4 or 5.

Projections and borders

  • The default is an orthographic projection (a globe), centred on the time zone of the viewer's device. No location is sent.
  • Equal Earth and Gall-Peters are equal-area. Mercator keeps shapes and enlarges high latitudes. The polar map is the azimuthal equidistant projection used in the UN emblem.
  • Borders are from Natural Earth (1:50 million), changed to match UN member states: Crimea in Ukraine, Northern Cyprus in Cyprus, Somaliland in Somalia.

Limits

  • Event data rely on media and local reports, so places with little access are undercounted.
  • UCDP candidate events are preliminary and can be revised in the yearly release.
  • ACLED includes violence by armed criminal groups, such as cartels in Mexico. Its free data is per province, so large provinces are shown around their centre.
  • GDELT is coded by machine and is not checked by people. One event can be counted more than once, places can be wrong (news is often placed where it was filed, such as a capital), and news volume follows media attention. The sample of 24 days per year can miss short events.
  • Correlates of War ends in 2014 and covers only conflicts between states, not civil wars.
  • Arrows show where a country's forces fought or were reported to act, not who started a war or whether it was lawful. In Correlates of War, side A took the first militarized action in a dispute, which is not the same as starting a war.
  • Travel advice measures risk to travellers, not the intensity of a conflict, and is mostly issued per country.
